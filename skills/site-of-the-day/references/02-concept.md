# 02 — The concept engine

A concept is not a style ("dark, minimal, editorial") and not a feature ("3D hero"). It is a **mechanism that turns the subject's meaning into something the visitor experiences**. Juries reward it under Creativity (20%) and it lifts Design and Content because every choice now has a reason.

## Step A — Raw material inventory
Fill this before thinking about layout. Be concrete; nouns and verbs, not adjectives.
- **Objects**: what physical things exist in this subject's world? (a webcam, a can of maté, a racing helmet, a jailhouse case file, a typeface's glyphs, an architectural drawing)
- **Verbs**: what do people *do*? (deliver, scan, draw, throw, annotate, ferment, render, sign)
- **Numbers**: what is measured? (lap times, kWh, years, dimensions, count of something)
- **Time**: is there a history, a process, a before/after, a countdown?
- **Senses**: sound, texture, temperature, light.
- **Tools & interfaces native to the subject**: a BIOS screen, a drafting table, a mixing desk, a passport, a receipt, a map, a contact sheet.
- **Tension**: what is the subject arguing against? (Zero University vs "the broken education system"; Flashlights vs a prison system.)

## Step B — Ten moves that produce winning concepts
Pick one. Combine at most two.

1. **Make the user perform the message.** The core interaction *is* the claim. *Why Zero* (SOTM 2026, C8.16): "Draw a zero" — you literally draw the brand's symbol to enter. *Paper Planes* (SOTY 2016): throw a plane from your phone. *Sephora Pinball*: the promotion is a game you play.
2. **Simulate the subject's machine.** The site pretends to be the instrument. *Shader Development Studio* (C8.33): the site boots like a CRT/BIOS ("Version 1.02", progress bar, scanlines) because they make real-time graphics. *Active Theory v6*: an OS with AI chat and multiplayer.
3. **Show the transformation.** The subject's value is a before→after; scroll performs it. *Heron AI*: an elevation line drawing renders into a shaded building — the product (an AI that works inside design tools) shown as a drawing becoming real. *Telkom-OT*: wireframe toolpath becomes solid machine part.
4. **Make the metaphor literal and spatial.** *Messenger* (SOTY 2025): "a small planet, someone's gotta make the deliveries" — you deliver mail on a tiny planet. *Umami Land* (SOTY 2021): a virtual food trip through Japanese dishes.
5. **The type is the image.** When the subject is typographic, cultural, or a strong name: set it so large it becomes architecture. *Exat* (the typeface tells its own story with glyph construction diagrams), *Siena Film Foundation* (one word on black, like a title card), *Bennett & Clive* (the client list *is* the hero, giant, over film stills), *Champions for Good* (22vw condensed).
6. **Turn the archive into the narrative.** History or data becomes chapters with their own color and pace. *Flashlights* (jailhouse lawyers): each period gets a color field; case files, quotes and archival photos are set like a magazine; timeline navigation along the bottom.
7. **Let the product be a character.** Portrait-level attention to one object or person. *Lando Norris* (SOTY 2025, 8.18): his face, his helmet in 3D, his handwritten signature as a neon stroke, one acid accent (#D2FF00). *Opal Tadpole*: the webcam on a real desk among the objects that inspired it.
8. **Build a world, not a page.** Explorable 3D with toy-like rules. *Bruno Simon* (8.11, C8.62): drive a car through the portfolio. *Leo Parpeix*: a sculpted studio room. Only when the subject benefits from exploration.
9. **Borrow a non-web format.** A title card, a pinball table, a museum label, a field guide, a liner note, a ticket, a manual, a contact sheet, a broadcast. The format brings its own grammar (and gets you out of landing-page grammar).
10. **Remove the expected and keep one thing.** A single-screen site with one perfect element. *Siena*: logotype + ENTER. Several top sites are 1 viewport (p25 page length = 1). Only works if that one thing is extraordinary.

## Step C — The sentence test
Write: **"The site is a ___ where you ___ to ___."**
- *"…a BIOS boot screen where you power on a studio to see what it renders."*
- *"…a drafting table where you watch a line drawing become a building to understand what the agent does."*
Then check:
- **Specificity**: swap in a competitor's name. If it still works, it's not a concept.
- **Verb**: is the visitor doing something (draw, drive, deliver, tune, assemble, scrub through time)? Doing beats watching.
- **Payoff**: does the mechanism make the subject's point better than a paragraph could?
- **Cost**: can it be built well in the time available? A modest idea executed perfectly beats an ambitious idea executed at 80%. Usability and Design (70% of score) punish half-built ambition.
- **Reversibility**: can the user skip it and still get the content? (Usability.)

## Step D — Translate the concept into every layer
Write one line each before building:
- **Signature interaction** (the thing a juror will describe): …
- **Navigation**: how does the concept change how you move? (Unusual Navigation is the #1 Creativity tag — but it must stay learnable: label it, provide a plain fallback index.)
- **Type**: what voice? (machine = mono/bitmap; archive = editorial; sport = condensed; culture = the subject's own lettering)
- **Color**: which two colors, and what does the accent *mean*?
- **Motion verb**: one verb for all motion (snap, slide, unfold, develop, boot, pour, print).
- **Sound** (optional, off by default, toggle visible): what does the world sound like?
- **Loading**: how does the preloader belong to the concept? (counter, boot, drawing, filling…)
- **404 / footer / easter egg**: winners extend the idea into the edges (Don't Board Me won SOTY with a 404 page).

## Concept anti-patterns
- "Immersive 3D experience showcasing our innovative solutions" — no mechanism.
- A mood ("cinematic, bold, minimal") presented as a concept.
- An effect looking for a reason: noise shader, cursor trail, magnetic buttons, liquid image hover, horizontal scroll — without tying to the subject.
- Two concepts competing. Cut one.
- A concept only visible after 30 seconds of scrolling. The first viewport must already state it.
